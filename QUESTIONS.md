## QUESTION-5
Section: §8.10 (Clock) with §13.4 (End of shift)
Context: §8.10 gives the clock as 09:00 + 8 h × k/n after the k-th of n tasks is sent. For the last task of a day (k = n) that is 17:00. §8.10 and §13.4 then set the clock to 16:58 when the End of Shift memo arrives. Days 1 to 3 have 3 tasks and Day 5 has 2.
Question: What should the clock show when the last task of a day is sent?
Options you see: A: 17:00 at that moment, then 16:58 when the End of Shift memo arrives (literal reading) / B: 16:58 at that moment, with no 17:00 step / C: something else

Decision (opus-agent): A — follow the literal reading. When the last task is sent, the hands animate to 17:00 over 2.0 s (§8.10 formula with k = n). When the End of Shift memo arrives, they animate back to 16:58 over 2.0 s (§8.10, §13.4). Both statements can be carried out exactly as written, so there is no contradiction to resolve. The slip back to 16:58 mirrors the 08:58 day start and is a deliberate-looking detail that B would remove by inventing an exception to the formula. On Days 3 and 4 the 16:58 step happens at the delayed End of Shift arrival (QUESTION-6). On Day 5 there is no End of Shift; D5-U2 freezes the clock when P-1D (task 1 of 2) is sent, so no further clock steps take effect.

Options you see: A: yes, the day-specific trigger replaces §13.4 on Days 3 and 4 / B: no, §13.4 applies on every day and the ghost-line trigger is ignored / C: something else

Decision (opus-agent): A — on Days 3 and 4, the §14.9 and §14.10 triggers replace the "5.0 s after the last task" timing in §13.4. The specific rule overrides the general one. ## QUESTION-8
Section: §6.4 (Desk items: read stack, carbon spot) and §8.5 (carbon spot stacking)
Context: §6.4 says the read stack opens "newest first", but does not say where a new document goes. §8.5 says carbons are "stacked on top" of the carbon spot, and §14.7 says a ghost sheet is placed "on top of the read stack". Implementation currently stores the read stack with the newest document at index 0, and the carbon spot with the newest at the end.
Question: What is the order of the read stack and the carbon spot?
Options you see: A: read stack newest at the top (index 0), carbon spot top is the last item added (current implementation) / B: both stacks newest at the end / C: something else

Decision (opus-agent): B — on both stacks, the item placed most recently is on top. Store it at the end of the array, so a new item is appended. Read view on either stack opens on the top item and steps toward older items ("newest first", §6.4). "Newest" means most recently placed on the stack, not the document's creation time. A single convention satisfies §6.4, §8.5 ("stacked on top") and §14.7 ("on top of the read stack") together. Two opposite conventions would invite off-by-order bugs.
Placeholder in code/config: matches the current code. `GameState.place()` in `scripts/autoload/game_state.gd` appends for every location, the read stack included; no index-0 insert exists in the scripts. ## QUESTION-10
Section: §7.2 (accepted characters) with §7.8 (accuracy) and §14.10/§14.8 (transcription sources)
Context: The C-22 transcription source (Task 1 of Day 2, §14.6) includes the line "CIRCULAR 22/C — ON THE KEEPING OF RECORDS", which contains an em dash (U+2014). §7.2 lists the only accepted characters: letters, digits, space and . , - ' / ? : ; ( ) & " !. The typewriter cannot produce an em dash, so a faithful copy can never reach accuracy 1.0. Accuracy is 1 − levenshtein(typed, source)/len(source), with both sides normalised (§7.8). The spec does not say how an unavailable character in the source is handled.
Question: How should the em dash in a transcription source be compared with what the player can type?
Options you see: A: the source keeps the em dash, so the player's best typing (a hyphen) costs one edit and the accuracy stays slightly below 1.0 (the implementer's test types a hyphen; this is the literal reading) / B: normalise the source's em dash to a hyphen before comparing, so a faithful copy scores 1.0 / C: normalise the em dash to a space / D: something else

Decision (opus-agent): A — follow the literal reading. The source keeps the em dash, and §7.8 normalisation stays exactly as specified (uppercase, collapse whitespace, trim). The em dash therefore costs one edit (about 0.003 on C-22's roughly 370 characters), so a faithful copy scores just under 1.0. The player never sees this: accuracy appears only through the morning-memo bands (§14.6), and the score stays far above the 0.98 ACCEPTABLE threshold. B and C would add a normalisation step the spec does not have. (There is no contradiction in the spec: §7.8 never promises that 1.0 is reachable.)
Placeholder in code/config: matches. `TextNorm.normalise()` and `TextNorm.accuracy()` in `scripts/logic/text_norm.gd` need no change. In `tests/unit/test_playthrough_compliant.gd`, expect the reduced value, not 1.0: the T-2 accuracy is exactly 1 − 1/len(normalised C-22 source), which is ≥ 0.98 and < 1.0. The current check (≥ 0.99) is acceptable. Optionally tighten it to `< 1.0`, and keep the M3 check for `TRANSCRIPTION T-2: ACCEPTABLE.`
