## 1. Overview

**Working title:** CARBON
**Genre:** First-person psychological horror / office simulation. No jump scares.
**Length:** 5 in-game workdays (Monday–Friday), roughly 60–90 minutes total.
**Platform:** Windows and Linux desktop (export presets for both). Keyboard and mouse only. No gamepad support.
**Engine:** Godot 4.3-stable, GDScript only.

**Premise (for the agent's understanding; this text never appears in the game):** The player is Clerk 0412 at Desk 4 in Hall C of the Bureau of Continuity, a registry in an unnamed city in an unspecified mid-20th-century period. The Bureau's records do not describe reality; they maintain it. What is redacted ceases to have existed. Over five days the player is asked to type, stamp and redact, and gradually the work turns toward people the player recognises, then toward the player. The horror is complicity, erasure of identity, and the unreliability of every record except the carbon copies the player chooses to keep.

**Core verbs:** READ, TYPE, STAMP, REDACT, SEND (by pneumatic tube), KEEP or FILE carbons.

## 2. Design pillars and hard constraints

### 2.1 Pillars
1. **Dread, never startle.** Distress comes from what the player notices, does and is complicit in, not from sudden events.
2. **The typewriter is the interface.** The player's own typing is the main act. What they type is recorded, returned, altered and used against them.
3. **Nothing changes while you watch.** The world changes only when unobserved (§9).
4. **The carbon is the only truth.** Carbon copies never change. Everything else may.
5. **Refusal is allowed and has a cost.** The game never forces compliance; refusal produces its own discomfort, not punishment.

### 2.2 Hard constraints (testable)
- **No jump scares.** Defined as: every sound obeys the loudness rules in §11.1 (non-player sounds peak ≤ −24 dBFS, and any above −30 dBFS have an attack ≥ 150 ms; the only exception is the tube arrival, always preceded by its 1.0 s rising whoosh); no object ever moves toward the camera; no object ever appears inside the camera frustum; no forced camera movement except the scripted transitions listed in this document; no screen flashes, no sudden full-screen images.
- **No death, no failure state, no game over, no chase, no combat, no timer pressure.** The only timed events are ambient (ghost typing, §10) and never require player reaction.
- **No faces.** No human face is ever rendered. Clerk heads are featureless (§5.4).
- **No music.** Only diegetic sound.
- **No HUD.** No crosshair, no prompts, no icons, no subtitles, no objective markers. Exceptions: the menu folder (§16) and the content note (§16.1).
- **No voice acting.**
- **Gender-neutral text.** The player character is never gendered.

## 4. Rendering pipeline (PS1 look)

### 4.1 Resolution
- The 3D world renders into a `SubViewport` at an **internal resolution of 320×240** in free-look mode.
- In **focus views** (typing view and read view, §6.3), the internal resolution switches to **640×480** for the 3D layer. Switch instantly when entering/leaving the view.
- Setting "Internal resolution: HIGH" (§16.3) forces 640×480 everywhere.
- The SubViewport texture is drawn to the window with **nearest-neighbour** filtering, scaled by the largest integer factor that fits; remaining space is centred with black bars. If no integer factor ≥1 fits, scale non-integer to fit, preserving 4:3.
- SubViewport MSAA off, screen-space AA off, debanding off.

### 4.2 Spatial shader `psx_spatial.gdshader` (applied to every 3D mesh)
- `render_mode vertex_lighting, cull_back, diffuse_lambert, specular_disabled;`
- **Vertex snapping:** in `vertex()`, transform to clip space, divide by w, snap x and y to a grid of the current internal resolution (`floor(ndc * res * 0.5) / (res * 0.5)` using 160×120 for 320×240 internal and 320×240 for 640×480), multiply back by w. The snap grid uniform is set globally when the resolution switches.
- **Affine texture mapping:** pass UV multiplied by clip-space w as a varying, plus w; divide in `fragment()`. Apply to every textured surface.
- **Texture filtering:** `filter_nearest`, no mipmaps.
- Uniforms: `albedo_tex` (sampler2D), `albedo_color` (vec4, default white), `uv_scale` (vec2, default 1,1), `emission_strength` (float, default 0).
- Max 4 lights affect any vertex (Godot default limit is fine).

### 4.3 Post shader `psx_post.gdshader` (on the full-screen quad that displays the SubViewport)
- **Colour depth:** quantise each channel to 5 bits (32 levels).
- **Dither:** before quantising, add an ordered 4×4 Bayer offset of amplitude `1/32` per channel, using the internal-resolution pixel coordinate. Setting "Dither: OFF" removes the offset but keeps quantisation.
- No CRT curvature, no scanlines, no chromatic aberration, no vignette, no film grain, no bloom.

### 4.4 Fog
Linear depth fog in the spatial shader: colour `#2A2A26`, start 6.0 m, end 18.0 m, max density 1.0.

### 4.5 Ambient
World environment ambient light `#3A3A34`, energy 1.0. No sky (clear colour behind everything; the hall is enclosed). No SSAO, SSR, SDFGI, glow.

### 4.6 Animation frame rate
All character (clerk figure) animation is **stepped at 15 fps** (hold each pose for 4 render frames at 60 fps). Object animations (drawers, typewriter carriage, keys, doors, levers, lamp switch, page movement, clock hands) are smooth at 60 fps. Camera movement is smooth.

### 6.2 Player controller
Two body states: **SEATED** (default) and **STANDING**.
- **SEATED:** camera at Desk 4 seated eye position. Mouse look: yaw clamped to ±110° from north, pitch clamped −60° to +40°. No translation.
- **STANDING:** press `Space` while seated and not in a focus view. Camera rises over 0.6 s (ease in-out) to standing eye height 1.62 m, 0.3 m south of the seated position. WASD move at 1.6 m/s, no running, no jumping, no crouching. Collision capsule radius 0.25, height 1.7. Head bob: vertical sine, amplitude 0.02 m, 1.8 Hz, only while moving. Mouse look: free yaw, pitch −70° to +70°. Footstep sound every 0.6 s while moving (§11.3).
- **Return to SEATED:** press `Space` while within 1.2 m of Desk 4's chair, or left-click Desk 4's chair. Camera moves over 0.8 s to the seated pose, yaw north.
- Desk 4 interactions (all items in §5.4, typewriter, tube) only work while SEATED. Standing interactions: door handles (§8.13), Desk 4 chair.
- Mouse sensitivity: setting (§16.3), default 0.6, mapped so 1.0 = 0.15° per pixel.
- Camera FOV 60° vertical, near 0.05, far 30.

### 6.3 Views
1. **Free view** — normal first-person (seated or standing).
2. **Typing view** — entered by left-clicking the typewriter while seated. Camera tweens over 0.5 s to a fixed pose looking down at the loaded paper (paper fills ~70% of the vertical view; copyholder visible at left). Mouse cursor visible. Keyboard input goes to the typewriter (§7). Holding **right mouse button** tweens the camera over 0.3 s to a close-up of the copyholder; releasing tweens back. `Esc` exits to free view (paper stays loaded). If no paper is loaded, clicking the typewriter does nothing.
3. **Read view** — entered by left-clicking any document (inbox tray, read stack, carbon spot, notebook, a document held in hand). The 3D view stays rendered behind; a full-screen `CanvasLayer` shows the document (§6.5) centred, 86% of window height, with a 50% black dim behind it. Mouse cursor visible. Mouse wheel scrolls documents taller than the view. `Left`/`Right` arrows (or `A`/`D`) page between pages of a multi-page document or between documents in a stack. `Esc` or right-click closes read view.

### 6.4 Interaction model
- **Cursor:** in free view the mouse is captured; the "pointer" is the screen centre. No crosshair is drawn.
- **Interactable highlight:** none. No outlines, no prompts.
- **Clicking:** left-click raycasts from the screen centre, max distance 1.6 m (seated) / 2.0 m (standing).
- **Held item:** the player holds at most one item. A held document is shown as a small paper quad in the lower-right of the view (3D, attached to the camera). A held stamp or marker or fluid bottle is shown similarly. **Right-click in free view** returns the held item to its home position.
- **Desk items:**
  | Click | Result |
  |---|---|
  | Inbox tray (with documents) | Opens read view on the top unread item. Closing read view moves that item to the read stack. |
  | Read stack | Opens read view on the stack (newest first). |
  | Blank paper tray | Picks up one blank sheet. If a transcription task (T-1 to T-4) is the active task, picks up a **sheet + carbon set** instead (§8.5). |
  | Typewriter while holding a sheet or form | Loads it into the typewriter (0.6 s animation: paper slides in, platen turns), then enters typing view. Only one paper can be loaded at a time; if one is loaded, the click enters typing view instead and the held item stays held. |
  | Copyholder while holding a document | Clips it to the copyholder (replacing and returning any previous one to the read stack). |
  | Copyholder (empty hand) | Opens read view on the clipped document. |
  | Stamp on rack | Picks up that stamp (§8.1). |
  | Marker | Picks up the marker (§8.2). |
  | Correction fluid | Picks up the bottle (§7.6). |
  | Tube receiver while holding a document | Sends it (§8.4). |
  | Carbon spot | Opens read view on the carbon stack. |
  | Top drawer | Opens (0.4 s slide 0.30 m south). Click again to close. Notebook clickable when open. |
  | Lower drawer | Opens/closes as above. While open, clicking the carbon spot **files** all carbons (§8.5) instead of reading them. |
  | Lamp | Ends the day if allowed (§13.5). Otherwise toggles nothing and plays the switch click only. |
- **Documents from the inbox and read stack can be picked up** with a click-and-hold of 0.4 s instead of a click (a click opens read view; a hold picks up). While holding a document, clicking the read stack puts it there.

### 6.5 Document rendering
- Every document is rendered into its own `SubViewport` texture at **768×1088 px** per page (A4 ratio), drawn on `paper.png` (or `onionskin.png` for carbons), and displayed both in read view and on 3D paper quads.
- Typed/printed text: Special Elite, size 22 px, monospaced grid of **64 columns × 54 lines**, left margin 40 px, top margin 48 px, column width 10.8 px, line height 18.6 px. Text is uppercase.
- Handwritten text: Homemade Apple, size 26 px, ragged lines, left margin 56 px, line height 34 px, ink `#1C1B19` with opacity 0.9. Preserve case as written in this spec.
- Typed glyph jitter (applies to player-typed and ghost-typed glyphs only; pre-printed text has no jitter): per glyph, x offset random ±0.3 px, y offset random ±0.6 px, opacity random 0.82–1.00. Stored per cell, so re-rendering is identical.
- **Overstrike:** a cell may hold up to 3 glyphs; each additional glyph is drawn on top with its own jitter. A 4th glyph in the same cell is ignored (no sound difference).
- **Redaction bars:** solid `#0E0E0E` rectangles, drawn above text.
- **Stamp impressions:** see §8.1.
- **Red Management corrections:** see §8.6.
- **Carbons** render the same cells as their original, in carbon ink colour with a 0.6 px blur and opacity ×0.85, on onionskin. Carbons are immune to all later changes (§8.7).
- **Text assist setting** (§16.3): in read view and typing view, an additional plain overlay panel to the right of the document shows the same text in Special Elite 28 px on `#E6DFC8`, ink `#1C1B19`, without jitter, redaction bars shown as `█` runs. Off by default.

## 7. The typewriter

### 7.1 Paper grid and carriage
- 64 columns × 54 lines per sheet. The carriage position is (line, column), starting at (0, 0) when a sheet is loaded, or at the first field of a form (§7.5).
- The visible carriage moves along X so the current column is always under the typing point: carriage offset = (32 − column) × 0.004 m (clamped to ±0.20 m when the paper is offset by the platen rotation).
- The platen rotates 360°/54 per line feed (visual only).

### 7.2 Accepted characters
Letters `A–Z` (lowercase input is converted to uppercase), digits `0–9`, space, and `. , - ' / ? : ; ( ) & " !`. Any other key does nothing and makes no sound. Input uses the `unicode` of `InputEventKey` (not physical keycodes), so keyboard layouts work. Key repeat is ignored (only `pressed && !echo` events).

### 7.3 Typing a character
1. Write the glyph into the current cell (overstrike if occupied, §6.5).
2. Advance column by 1.
3. Animate the matching key: depress 0.004 m over 30 ms, return over 60 ms. Also animate a typebar flick: none (not modelled).
4. Play `key_clack` (§11.3) at the typewriter, pitch random ×0.92–1.08, volume random −2 to 0 dB.
5. Send the event to `Cadence` (§10.1) if a task paper or ghost sheet is loaded. Free sheets also count.
6. If the column becomes 58, play `bell`.
7. At column 64 the carriage is at the margin: further characters do nothing except play `key_jam` (§11.3).

### 7.4 Other keys
| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Enter` | Carriage return: carriage slides back to column 0 over 0.35 s (`carriage_return` sound), line +1. At line 53, `Enter` plays `key_jam` and does nothing. |
| `Backspace` | Moves the carriage back one column (min 0). **Does not erase.** Plays `backspace_click`. |
| `Up` / `Down` | Platen knob: line −1 / +1 (clamped 0–53). Plays `platen_ratchet`. Column unchanged. |
| `Left` / `Right` | Nothing. |
| `Tab` | Nothing. |
| `Esc` | Exit typing view. |

### 7.5 Forms
- Forms are pre-printed documents with fields (§14.4 notation). When a form is loaded, the carriage starts at the first field's first cell.
- **Clicking a cell inside a field** (left-click on the paper in typing view) moves the carriage to that field's start, animating the carriage over 0.4 s with `platen_ratchet` for each line passed.
- Typing inside a field is the same as on blank paper, except the carriage cannot advance past the field's last cell: extra characters play `key_jam` and do nothing. `Enter` inside a form moves to the next field's start (in reading order); from the last field, `Enter` does nothing.
- A field's **value** is read as the field's cells left to right, where each cell's character is its **last** glyph (overstruck cells use the last glyph typed), whited-out cells are spaces, then trimmed and with internal runs of spaces collapsed to one.

### 7.6 Correction fluid
- 12 uses for the entire game (stored in `GameState.fluid_uses`, starts at 12). When 0, the bottle can be picked up but does nothing. There is no visual counter.
- While holding the bottle in typing view, left-click a cell: paint a blob of correction fluid colour covering that cell (a 12×20 px rounded rectangle with 3 px irregular edges, generated per use from the RNG), clear all glyphs in the cell, and decrement uses. The cell cannot receive glyphs for 2.0 s (key presses at that cell play `key_jam`). After drying, typing writes over the blob.
- Play `fluid_brush` on each use.
- Right-click returns the bottle to its spot.

### 7.7 Removing paper
In typing view, left-click the paper release lever: the paper slides up and out over 0.6 s (`paper_out` sound) and the player now holds it; the view returns to free view. If it was a sheet + carbon set, the original goes to the player's hand and the carbon is placed automatically onto the carbon spot (§8.5).

### 7.8 Accuracy measurement (transcriptions)
- Source text: the transcription source as specified in §14 (after token substitution), normalised.
- Typed text: all cells of the typed page, read line by line (last glyph per cell, whiteout = space), lines joined with single spaces, normalised.
- **Normalise:** uppercase; replace any run of whitespace with one space; trim.
- Accuracy = `max(0, 1 − levenshtein(typed, source) / len(source))`. Store as a float per transcription in `GameState`.

### 7.9 Free sheets
- The player may take blank sheets at any time (tray holds 10 per day, refilled to 10 at each day start; the tray mesh shows a stack whose height = 0.002 m × remaining count; empty tray does nothing on click).
- Free sheets may be typed on, kept, put on the read stack, or sent by tube. A sent free sheet is returned the next morning stamped `NO SUCH ADDRESSEE` (§8.4). Free sheets sent on Day 5 are not returned.

### 7.10 The retired word (from Day 3)
See §8.8.

## 8. Systems

### 8.1 Stamps
- Pick up a stamp from the rack. In read view on a stampable document (applications S-*, redaction orders RO-*), left-click the document: play `stamp_thud`, render the impression centred at the click point, rotated random −6° to +6°, opacity random 0.75–0.95.
- **Impression:** a rectangle 260 × 70 px with a 4 px border, the word centred in Special Elite 34 px, ink colour per §5.2, with 20% of pixels in the shape randomly knocked out to opacity 0.3 (seeded per impression). Words: `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `PROCESSED`, `RETURNED — UNPROCESSED` (this last one at 24 px to fit).
- Pressing the stamp on the ink pad is not required.
- The **first** stamp impression on each document (or on each page of an application batch) determines its recorded result. Later impressions are drawn but do not change the result; if a later impression differs from the first, increment `GameState.contradictory_stamps`.
- Stamps work only in read view. Right-click in read view while holding a stamp returns it to the rack (does not close read view); a second right-click closes read view.

### 8.2 Redaction marker
- Pick up the marker. In read view on a redaction order document, **left-click-and-drag horizontally** on the document: a black bar is drawn from the press point to the release point, height 16 px, vertically centred on the text line nearest the press point. Bars are permanent; there is no undo. Play `marker_stroke` (length matched to drag duration, max 1.2 s) while dragging.
- Bars can be drawn on any line of a redaction order, including lines not listed for redaction and the order text itself.
- An **entry is redacted** if the union of bars on its line covers at least 80% of the horizontal extent of the entry's name text.
- Right-click returns the marker as with stamps.

### 8.3 Redaction world effects
- When a redaction order is sent, record for each listed entry: `redacted` (true/false). Also record unlisted entries that were redacted (store but no effect).
- **From the next day onward**, every occurrence of a redacted name (and its aliases, §14.3) in any **non-carbon** document — including documents already on the desk, the notebook, returned originals and the applications in later batches — renders as a black bar of the occurrence's width. Text assist shows `█` runs.
- Clerk-specific effects (desks 1–11, and H. VANCE) are in §8.9.
- Citizen entries refused on RO-2 are **carried forward** to RO-4 (§14.7).

### 8.4 Pneumatic tube
- **Sending:** click the receiver while holding a document. The flap opens (0.2 s), the paper is drawn in, flap closes, `tube_send` plays (whoosh rising then falling, 0.8 s), canister travels up and out of sight. The document leaves the game world.
- **Which documents can be sent:** task outputs, task documents, free sheets, carbons. Morning memos, cover sheets that are not stamped/typed outputs, and returned documents **can** also be sent; they simply disappear and are counted in `GameState.misc_sent`. Nothing is ever refused.
- **Task completion:** a task completes when its designated output document is sent (§13.3). Anything about the output (blank, wrong, unstamped) is recorded, never blocked.
- **Arrivals:** `tube_arrive_whoosh` (1.0 s rising whoosh, starting at −36 dBFS rising to −26 dBFS), then the canister drops into the receiver with `tube_thunk` (peak −20 dBFS), the flap opens, and the arrived documents move to the inbox tray (0.5 s slide). Canister then leaves with `tube_send` at −30 dBFS.
- **Returned free mail:** each free sheet sent on day N (N ≤ 4) arrives in the next morning's canister, unchanged, with a red `NO SUCH ADDRESSEE` impression (same style as stamps, red ink, 30 px text) centred in the top third.

### 8.5 Carbons
- When a sheet + carbon set is in use, every glyph, whiteout and overstrike on the original is mirrored to the carbon in the same cell.
- On removal (§7.7), the carbon goes to the carbon spot, stacked on top.
- **Filing:** with the lower drawer open, click the carbon spot: all carbons currently on the spot move into the drawer (0.6 s, `paper_shuffle`). Filed carbons are **removed from the game overnight** (at the next day start, the drawer is empty).
- **Keeping:** any carbon left on the carbon spot remains there permanently (across days) unless later filed.
- Carbons can be picked up (hold-click) and moved to the read stack or copyholder; a carbon not on the carbon spot or in the drawer at end of day counts as kept and is returned to the carbon spot overnight.
- `GameState.carbons_kept_at_final` is evaluated at the moment RO-5 is sent: the number of **transcription carbons (T-1, T-2, T-3, T-4)** currently in the world outside the lower drawer.
- **Carbons never change.** No drift, no Management corrections, no redaction bars, no retired-word bars, no substitution (§8.7).

### 8.6 Management corrections (returned originals)
Some typed originals are returned the next morning with red corrections (§14.8 lists them). Rendering rule for each correction `{find, replace}`:
1. Normalise the player's typed page text into a single string with a map from string index back to cells.
2. Normalise `find` the same way.
3. Slide a window of `len(find)` across the typed string; similarity = `1 − levenshtein(window, find) / len(find)`. Pick the best window (earliest on ties).
4. If best similarity ≥ 0.6: draw a 2 px red horizontal strike line through the centre of every cell in that window (spanning lines if needed), and write `replace` in red typed glyphs on the half-line above the first cell of the window, continuing to the right; if it would pass column 63, continue on the next half-line at column 0.
5. Otherwise: write `ADD: ` + `replace` in red typed glyphs on the first empty line below the last typed line.
Red glyphs have the same jitter as typed glyphs.

### 8.7 Immunity rules
Carbons are immune to: redaction world effects, retired-word bars, Management corrections, Day-5 name substitution (§14.12), document drift (§8.14). Everything else is subject to them.

### 8.8 The retired word
- `{FOND_WORD}` is set from Form P-1 field 3 (§14.5).
- From the start of Day 3, every `{FOND_WORD}` token occurrence in game text, and every whole-word occurrence of it in player-typed or ghost-typed text on non-carbon pages, renders as a black bar.
- From the start of Day 3, the typewriter tracks the current run of letters typed on any paper (any non-letter key, including space and Enter, ends the run). When a run ends and it equals `{FOND_WORD}`:
  1. Play `key_jam`, then `bell` twice 0.3 s apart.
  2. Lock typewriter input for 3.0 s (key presses play `key_jam` only).
  3. During the lock, ghost-type `X` over each cell of the just-typed word, right to left, at 120 ms per cell, each with `key_clack`. These X glyphs overstrike. The carriage moves to each cell as it is struck, then returns to its pre-lock position over 0.35 s with `carriage_return`.
- This can occur any number of times.

## 9. Gaze and the Unseen Change system

### 9.1 Gaze definitions (computed every frame by `Gaze`)
For each tracked node (any node referenced by an unseen change, the typewriter, each clerk, the clock, the supervisor door glass, Desk 12):
- **In frustum:** the node's world AABB intersects the camera frustum.
- **Looked at:** in frustum AND the angle between the camera forward vector and the direction to the AABB centre is ≤ 12° AND a physics raycast from the camera to the AABB centre is not blocked by another collider (the node's own collider is ignored).
- **Unseen time:** seconds since the node was last in frustum (0 while in frustum).
- **Seen time:** continuous seconds the node has been looked at (resets to 0 when not looked at).
Read view and typing view count as **not** looking at anything except the paper/typewriter; nodes behind the overlay are treated as out of frustum while read view is open.

### 9.2 Unseen change rules
- Each change has: `id`, `day`, `after` (a trigger: `day_start` or `task_sent:<task_id>`), `target`, `operation`, and optional `revert` rule.
- A change becomes **pending** when its trigger fires. It is **applied** on the first frame where the target's unseen time ≥ 1.5 s AND the target's distance from the camera ≥ 2.0 m.
- `day_start` changes are applied during the black day-transition screen (always safe).
- Applying a change never plays a sound unless stated.
- If the day ends with a change still pending, apply it during the transition.

## 10. Cadence capture and ghost typing

### 10.1 Capture
- For every accepted typing keypress (§7.3, §7.4 `Enter` included) on any paper, record the timestamp in milliseconds.
- **Interval** = time since the previous recorded keypress, if ≤ 1500 ms; longer gaps are not recorded (but still update "previous").
- Maintain:
  - `intervals`: ring buffer of the last 2000 intervals (clamped to 40–1500 ms).
  - `bigram_avg`: dictionary from a two-character string (previous char + current char, using `⏎` for Enter) to running mean interval and count.
  - `return_avg`: running mean interval of `Enter` presses.
- Persist all three in the save.

### 10.2 Ghost typing playback
- To ghost-type a string: for each character, wait a delay then type it into the target sheet exactly as §7.3 (sound, key animation, carriage movement; jitter applied), without recording to `Cadence`.
- Delay for a character = `bigram_avg[prev+curr]` if its count ≥ 2; else a random sample from `intervals`; if `intervals` is empty, 180 ms.
- Newline in a ghost string = `Enter` with delay `return_avg` (or 600 ms if none).
- Ghost typing runs only while its stop condition is false; when stopped mid-string it resumes from the same character on the next trigger.

### 10.3 Ghost sheet triggers (Days 3 and 4)
While a ghost sheet is loaded and the player is not in typing view:
- **Trigger** when either: the typewriter's unseen time ≥ 6.0 s, or the player's distance from the typewriter > 4.0 m for ≥ 3.0 s continuously.
- On trigger, ghost-type the next unfinished line from the day's list (below), followed by `Enter`.
- **Stop condition:** the typewriter enters the frustum. Stop immediately (after the current keystroke completes).
- Each line is typed at most once. After all lines are typed, no more ghost typing occurs that day.
- Day 3 lines:
  1. `IS THIS HOW YOU TYPE`
  2. `{PLAYER_NAME} TYPES LIKE THIS`
  3. `I LEARNED IT FROM YOU`

## 11. Audio

### 11.1 Buses and loudness rules
- Buses: `Master` → `SFX`, `Ambient`. Only Master volume is exposed in settings.
- All files: 44.1 kHz, 16-bit, mono, WAV. Peak levels below are the file's normalised peak; playback levels in this section are additional gain at the player's position.
- **Player-caused** means triggered by a player input, or a scripted direct consequence of one that begins within 3.5 s of it (carriage return after `Enter`, the retired-word jam and bells, tube send after a click).
- **Rule A (no startles):** every sound that is not player-caused must peak at the listener ≤ −24 dBFS. If such a sound peaks above −30 dBFS, its attack must be ≥ 150 ms. One exception: `tube_thunk` (−20 dBFS), which is always preceded by `tube_arrive_whoosh`.
- **Ghost gain:** every `key_clack`, `carriage_return` and `bell` produced by ghost typing (§10.2, §15) plays with −20 dB additional gain, so it stays at or below −30 dBFS at Desk 4.
- **Rule B:** player-caused sounds peak ≤ −10 dBFS.
- **Rule C:** ambient beds sum to ≈ −34 dBFS RMS at Desk 4 with all fixtures on.
- Implement a debug check (debug builds only) that logs any playback violating Rules A/B.
- 3D sounds use `AudioStreamPlayer3D`, attenuation inverse distance, unit size 2.0, max distance 20 m, no doppler.

## 13. Day flow

### 13.1 Day transition (between days and at new game)
1. Fade to black over 2.0 s (skip at new game; the content note and title already are black).
2. Hold 1.0 s.
3. Type the day title centred on the black screen in Special Elite 40 px, colour `#C8C2AE`, one character every 140 ms, each with `key_clack`: `MONDAY`, `TUESDAY`, `WEDNESDAY`, `THURSDAY`, `FRIDAY`.
4. Hold 2.0 s, clear.
5. Run overnight resolution (§14.7) — not on Monday.
6. Autosave (§16.4).
7. Fade in over 2.0 s with the player SEATED at Desk 4, yaw north, pitch −10°, lamp on.

### 13.2 Day start sequence
- t = 0 (fade-in complete): clock shows 08:58.
- t = 4.0 s: `start_bell` (non-positional). Clock hands animate to 09:00.
- t = 10.0 s: the **morning canister** arrives (§8.4) with that day's morning documents (§14.5–§14.11).
- 5.0 s after the morning canister's documents reach the inbox: the first task canister arrives.

### 13.3 Task sequence
- Each task arrives in its own canister, containing the documents listed for it. A task becomes **active** on arrival.
- A task **completes** when its designated output document is sent by tube. The next task's canister arrives 5.0 s later.
- The clock advances on each completion (§8.10).
- The player may read, ignore, or send anything at any time; only the output send completes the task.

### 13.4 End of shift (Days 1–4)
5.0 s after the last task completes, a canister arrives with the day's End of Shift memo (§14). The clock moves to 16:58.

### 13.5 Ending the day
- Clicking the lamp after the End of Shift memo has arrived: `lamp_click`, lamp light off, shade emission off. After 1.5 s, begin the day transition (§13.1).
- Clicking the lamp before then: `lamp_click` only; the lamp stays on.
- Any carbons on the carbon spot remain there (kept). Any carbons in the open or closed lower drawer are filed (§8.5).
- Day 5 has no End of Shift; it ends with an ending (§15).

## 21. Debug tools (debug builds only; stripped from release exports)
F9 opens a one-line console. Commands:
- `day N` — jump to the start of day N with plausible default state (all prior orders processed, carbons filed, default tokens).
- `set <var> <value>` — set a GameState variable.
- `skip` — complete the active task with its output as-is (sends it).
- `gaze` — toggle an overlay listing tracked nodes with in-frustum, looked-at, unseen time.
- `changes` — list pending and applied unseen changes.
- `ending A|B|C` — jump to the start of an ending with current state.
- `audio_check` — toggle the Rule A/B loudness logger.
- `tris` — print the visible triangle count.
None of these may appear, be referenced, or be reachable in release builds.
